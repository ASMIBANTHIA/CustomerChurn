# Customer Churn Analysis and Retention Prioritization

An end-to-end notebook analysis of telecom customer churn: data cleaning, exploratory analysis, a logistic regression baseline, statistical comparisons, and an illustrative retention targeting exercise.

The analysis covers **7,043 customers**, of whom **1,869 (26.54%)** are recorded as having churned. The saved notebook reports **80.6% test accuracy** and identifies customer segments with elevated observed churn. It also ranks **5,174 non-churned customers** and selects **100** under an assumed contact budget. These are analytical results; no retention campaign was run and no reduction in churn or financial return was measured.

## Cleaning and analysis workflow

1. Inspect dimensions, types, and customer records.
2. Identify **11 blank `TotalCharges` values**, all on customers with zero tenure and `Churn = No`.
3. Convert `TotalCharges` to numeric and assign those 11 values to zero, assuming no accumulated charges. Retain all 7,043 customers.
4. Confirm **zero remaining missing values** and **zero duplicate customer IDs** after cleaning.
5. Compare churn across contracts, tenure groups, internet service, and support subscriptions; examine combined segments and charge distributions.
6. Train and evaluate a logistic regression baseline on a stratified holdout.
7. Test selected categorical and numerical associations, with effect sizes, confidence intervals, and multiple-testing adjustment.
8. Create summary charts and rank eligible customers for an illustrative outreach pilot.

Median tenure was **10 months for churned customers** and **38 months for non-churned customers**. This makes early-tenure, month-to-month customers a useful segment for investigating retention needs.

### Internet service, support, and charges

| Internet service | Churn rate |
| --- | ---: |
| DSL | 19.0% |
| Fiber optic | 41.9% |
| No internet service | 7.4% |

Within fiber optic customers, churn was **49.4% without tech support** versus **22.6% with tech support**. The combined fiber optic, month-to-month, no-tech-support segment contained **1,796 customers** and had **57.5% churn**.

Across the full dataset, mean monthly charges were **74.44 for churned customers** versus **61.27 for non-churned customers**. However, within fiber optic customers, median charges were **87.55 for churned customers** versus **94.80 for non-churned customers**. This difference between overall and subgroup patterns cautions against interpreting price alone as a cause of churn.

## Logistic regression baseline

The model uses 19 original features, excluding `customerID`, `Churn`, and the derived `tenure_group`. A stratified **80/20 split**, with `random_state=42`, creates **5,634 training** and **1,409 test** records.

Categorical features are one-hot encoded using `handle_unknown="ignore"` and `sparse_output=False`; four numerical features (`SeniorCitizen`, `tenure`, `MonthlyCharges`, `TotalCharges`) are standardized. Encoder and scaler are fitted on training data only and then applied to the test data. The resulting matrices contain **45 features**: 41 encoded categorical columns plus four numerical columns. Logistic regression uses `max_iter=1000` and otherwise default settings.

| Always predict non-churn: test accuracy | 73.46% |

Precision, recall, F1, and the majority-class baseline above are calculated from the saved confusion matrix; the notebook directly prints training/test accuracy. Values may vary slightly with library versions.

The baseline correctly identified **209 of 374 churned test customers**, while missing **165** and flagging **109 non-churned customers**. Its accuracy exceeds the majority-class baseline, but its recall shows that many churn cases remain undetected. The notebook also produces churn probabilities and displays the ten highest-ranked test records; it does not evaluate probability calibration, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, or alternative classification thresholds.

## Statistical comparisons

Four chi-square tests compare categorical attributes with churn. Two Welch t-tests compare mean monthly charges and tenure between churned and non-churned customers. Holm adjustment is applied jointly to these **six tests**; all six remain significant at the 0.05 level.

| Attribute | Test | Cramér's V | Holm-adjusted p-value |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Contract | Chi-square | 0.410 | 3.52e-257 |
| InternetService | Chi-square | 0.322 | 2.87e-159 |
| TechSupport | Chi-square | 0.343 | 5.77e-180 |
| SeniorCitizen | Chi-square | 0.150 | 1.51e-36 |

Contract has the largest Cramér's V among the four categorical attributes tested. All minimum expected counts exceed 303, supporting the expected-count condition for these chi-square calculations. Contract's separate test reports χ² = **1,184.60** and an unadjusted p-value of **5.86e-258**.

| Numerical attribute | Mean: churned | Mean: non-churned | Difference (churned − non-churned) | 95% CI for difference | Holm-adjusted p-value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| MonthlyCharges | 74.44 | 61.27 | +13.18 | [11.77, 14.58] | 1.72e-72 |
| tenure (months) | 17.98 | 37.57 | −19.59 | [−20.69, −18.49] | 5.98e-232 |

These are univariate comparisons. Statistical significance does not establish causality, independence from other attributes, or the effectiveness of a retention intervention. Holm adjustment covers these six tests, not every exploratory comparison in the notebook.

## Illustrative retention prioritization

The targeting exercise uses **observed contract × tenure-group churn rates**, independently of the logistic regression probabilities.

```text
risk_proxy = observed churn rate for the customer's contract/tenure segment
priority_score = risk_proxy × MonthlyCharges
```

Segments with fewer than 30 customers would use the overall churn rate as a fallback. All 12 observed contract/tenure segments contain at least 68 customers, so the fallback is not activated in this dataset. Only customers with `Churn = No` are eligible. They are sorted by descending priority score, with customer ID as an ascending tie-breaker.

| Targeting outcome / assumption | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Eligible customers ranked | 5,174 |
| Assumed budget | 1,000 monetary units |
| Assumed cost per contact | 10 monetary units |
| Contact capacity and customers selected | 100 |
| Assumed total contact cost | 1,000 monetary units |
| Selected customers' combined monthly charges | 9,128.75 monetary units |

The **9,128.75** figure is the selected customers' combined monthly billing amount. It is **not measured revenue saved, expected savings, campaign ROI, or incremental retention**. The score is a descriptive prioritization heuristic, not a calibrated individual future churn probability.

## Recommendations and next steps

- Pilot proactive outreach for higher-priority customers, especially early-tenure customers on month-to-month contracts.
- Investigate service quality, onboarding, billing clarity, and support needs through customer feedback before choosing an intervention.
- Evaluate an outreach pilot against a randomly assigned control group, measuring subsequent churn, incremental retention, contact cost, and net financial benefit.
- Improve model assessment with cross-validation, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC, probability calibration, and threshold selection based on the cost of missed churn and unnecessary outreach.
- Validate on a later time period and compare against simple baselines before operational use.

## Limitations

- **Sample and provenance:** Findings apply to the supplied sample. Collection dates, sampling design, currency, and redistribution rights are not documented in the local notebook; generalization to another provider or period is untested.
- **Associations and confounding:** Contract, tenure, internet service, and support subscriptions can be related. Univariate results do not isolate causal effects or prove that adding support or changing contracts reduces churn.
- **Cleaning assumption:** Replacing 11 blank total charges with zero is reasonable for zero-tenure records but remains an assumption; no sensitivity analysis was performed.
- **Evaluation scope:** Results come from one stratified random holdout. There is no temporal validation, cross-validation, hyperparameter search, uncertainty interval for model performance, or deployment evaluation.
- **Class imbalance:** Only 26.54% of records are churned. Accuracy alone is insufficient; the model misses 44.12% of actual churn cases in the test set.
- **Probability and timing:** Logistic regression probabilities have not been calibrated. Feature availability at a real prediction date and possible temporal leakage have not been audited.
- **Targeting validation:** Segment rates are calculated on the complete sample and used to rank non-churned records in that same sample. This is not an out-of-sample forecast or evidence of campaign benefit. Zero observed churn in a segment does not imply zero future risk.
- **Financial assumptions:** Monthly charges approximate billing value rather than profit or lifetime value. The budget and contact cost are illustrative; intervention effectiveness, discount costs, margins, and campaign response are unknown.
- **Fairness:** The model includes demographic information, including gender and senior-citizen status. Subgroup error rates and fairness have not been evaluated.
- **Reproducibility:** The requirements specify compatible ranges, not the exact original environment. Saved notebook results have not been independently rerun as part of this documentation update.
